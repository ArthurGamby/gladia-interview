# Part 2 · My first 90 days as Gladia's first DevRel

**In one line:** I learn the product as a new developer, fix what slows people down, and publish every week. Every piece leads to the docs, an account and a first transcription.

**What I do not know yet:** Gladia's analytics (most and least visited pages, sign-up funnel). I ask for them in week 1 and adjust this plan.

## 1. Positioning

**Gladia: speech-to-text that works in five minutes and holds up in production.** My job is to make both halves true, and visible.

- **Who I talk to.** First, developers and SaaS builders who add voice to a product (meeting assistants, voice agents, contact-center and sales tools). They bring the usage, so the revenue. Second, people who build with coding agents and automation tools. They bring the audience, and Gladia already has an MCP server, a CLI and an n8n node for them.
- **Where.** International, in English: Europe, where Gladia is at home, as much as the US. I read the numbers by country and adjust.
- **How it sounds.** A developer talking to developers, who teaches before selling. I post in English as Arthur, DevRel at Gladia, and Gladia's account reposts. We show real output and say what is not ready yet, as in [Part 1](../README.md).
- **Why now.** The product is ahead of its voice: the five latest videos have 97 to 410 views, the five latest X posts 34 to 121.

## 2. Pillars

1. **Make the first five minutes work (activation).** I go through the site, the docs and every feature as a new developer and note each snag. Fixes go in as pull requests; my first one has been open since 8 November. Then a "Get started" playlist, which Gladia does not have: Gladia in five minutes, then a short video for the most visited docs pages only. When a page gets no visits, I first find out why. *Why:* ElevenLabs does exactly this: a setup guide at 75K views, then one video per feature at 1.6K to 22K.
2. **Publish every week where developers look (awareness).** One video a week once the format is found, in three more playlists: "Build with voice AI" (projects with big names, like a note-taker built in three prompts with Claude Code), "Voice AI explained" (the domain, without selling) and "What's new". Nobody searches for a name they do not know, so "Gladia" is in the title only for "Get started" and "What's new". Elsewhere the topic or the big name leads, and Gladia is the tool inside. On X, one or two posts a day, visual first: a feature in one scroll, code with its JSON, a small fun app. *Why:* teaching the domain is the best advertising. One trailer aside, AssemblyAI's three most-viewed videos are about AI in general (632K to 775K views), and most of its product videos get about 1K. PlanetScale does the same with databases, Finary with money.
3. **Pair Gladia with the tools developers already use (awareness to adoption).** "Gladia + X" tutorials, each one a docs page, a sample and a video. I start with the 12 partners already in the docs, where most pages are a short description and a link, and test two or three first to see which name brings the most traction. Then developer names such as Next.js, Supabase and the Vercel AI SDK. I ask each partner to share. *Why:* a big name brings its audience: AssemblyAI's LiveKit tutorial has 39K views.
4. **Bring the community in (adoption).** Every comment and question gets an answer within 48 hours, with a question back, so it becomes a conversation. Every five to six weeks, an AMA: I collect the questions beforehand and put them to an engineer (or answer myself), on a call at first, at the office later. As often as possible, "You asked": I take a question or a complaint to the engineers and come back in public with a fix, a place on the roadmap, an explanation, or at least an answer. I talk with support and engineering every week, so each pain point becomes a fix and a piece of content. *Why:* developers stay where someone answers fast.

**Under all four, a weekly watch:** what competitors (AssemblyAI, Deepgram, ElevenLabs) ship and publish, and what is new in AI, in general and in voice. It tells me what to make while the topic is hot.

**Not in the first 90 days**

- **Speaking at conferences.** If the team runs one, I go to see how it is done.
- **A podcast, webinars, an ambassador program.** Too heavy for one person, and not what brings the most.
- **Promotion on Reddit.** I answer questions there, nothing else.

## 4. Distribution

- **Where a piece starts:** a pain point, a question, a release, or the weekly watch.
- **One video, many pieces:** two or three Shorts, two or three posts and, when it is useful, a docs embed or a technical blog post.
- **The loop:** posts and Shorts lead to the video, the video to the docs, the docs to an account in a click or two. Links are tagged, so we see what activates.
- **Channels:** YouTube is the library people search, X the daily conversation (my account and Gladia's). LinkedIn, Bluesky and Threads get an adapted copy through Typefully. Marketing's blog (254 posts) keeps doing search.
- **Creators:** Gladia already pays creators through its Creator Program. From day 61 I help with topics, product checks on drafts and YouTubers who fit.

Twelve weeks of content ideas are in [CONTENT-IDEAS.md](CONTENT-IDEAS.md).

## 5. Metrics

I do not know sign-ups or activation yet, so **every target is an assumption**. I do not chase subscribers or followers (YouTube recommends a video whether or not people subscribe): I look at views and engagement.

- **Leading, weekly:** YouTube views, likes, comments and clicks on description links; X views, bookmarks, reposts and replies; questions answered within 48 hours; doc fixes merged.
- **Lagging, monthly:** sign-ups from tagged links (YouTube, X, partners); accounts that make a first transcription; accounts still active four weeks later.
- **Success at 6 months (assumptions):** 15 to 20 videos live, each new one above 1,000 views (today 97 to 410); posts in developer formats at five times the views of today's posts (34 to 121); 10 "Gladia + X" tutorials published; first-transcription rate 20% above today's baseline; an AMA every five to six weeks.

## 6. Internal dependencies

- **Marketing:** slots on Gladia's X and YouTube, and the launch calendar. I test new post formats on my own X account (never on my own YouTube channel) and in a few slots a week on Gladia's; we keep what beats today's numbers.
- **Engineering:** quick reviews of docs pull requests, answers to community questions, an engineer for the AMA when one is free, and help on technical videos (a script check or a place on camera).
- **Support:** a weekly talk about what users ask and where they get stuck.
- **Product:** what we may say about the roadmap (I found a changelog, no public roadmap), release dates early enough to tease, and one listing question: OpenRouter has pages for AssemblyAI, Deepgram and ElevenLabs, not for Gladia.
- **Data:** docs and funnel analytics, by page and by country.
- **Leadership:** whether joining OVH Groupe adds an approval step, and a small tools budget (Typefully).
