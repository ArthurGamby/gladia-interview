Four lessons: a get-started series works, big names in the title work, teaching the domain beats showing the product, and a name nobody knows yet does not earn the click. Volume alone does not work.

## The title rule

"Gladia" goes in the title only when the viewer already looks for Gladia: "Get started" and "What's new". Everywhere else the title leads with the topic or a big name, and Gladia is the tool used in the video. We are too small for our name to earn the click. That changes as the channel grows.

## Posts (X first, adapted for LinkedIn, Bluesky and Threads)

Every post carries a visual: a short clip, a GIF, a formatted JSON, a clean terminal command. People stop on what moves. Text-only posts are the exception.

| Format | Example |
| --- | --- |
| A feature in one scroll | A 15-second screen recording: one CLI command, the transcript appears |
| Code and JSON | "Five lines to get speaker labels", with the response |
| Before and after | `code_switching: false` next to `true` (Part 1) |
| Built in three prompts | A small fun app that makes people stop scrolling |
| One idea explained | Why transcripts get names wrong, in one picture. No product |
| News of the week | From the watch: a new model or tool is out, and what you can build with it today. Only when it fits |
| A question to the community | "What is the one thing you wish speech-to-text did better?" |
| You asked | A question or a complaint, and what the engineers answered |
| Teaser | A few days before a release |

Each video also gets two or three posts that send people to it. From my own X account: the same things with more of the kitchen (what I am building, what broke, what I learned).

## Community

- An answer to every comment and question within 48 hours, on X, Discord and YouTube, with a question back
- Open questions, every week
- An AMA every five to six weeks: questions collected beforehand, put to an engineer in a short interview (on a call at first, at the office later)
- "You asked", as often as possible: a fix, a place on the roadmap, an explanation, or at least an answer
- A weekly talk with support and engineering, to hear about pain points as they appear
- A small challenge once the rhythm is there: build something with voice AI, the best project gets a video

## The weekly watch

- **Competitors:** what AssemblyAI, Deepgram and ElevenLabs ship (models, features, prices) and publish (videos, posts, docs)
- **AI in general:** the new models and tools everyone talks about
- **Voice AI:** new models, benchmarks, voice-agent frameworks
- **What comes out of it:** a "news of the week" post when it fits, and topics for "Build with voice AI" and "Voice AI explained" while they are hot. ElevenLabs has a full playlist built this way.
